### Phase 1: The Verb Engine (45 Minutes)
In Spanish, the verb ending tells you *who* is doing the action. We will focus on the **Present Tense** of regular **-AR** verbs (the largest group of verbs).

**The Concept:** Take a verb like **Hablar** (to speak). Drop the **-ar** and add these endings:
* **-o** (I): *Habl**o*** (I speak)
* **-as** (You): *Habl**as*** (You speak)
* **-a** (He/She/It): *Habl**a*** (He/She speaks)

**Action:** 1.  Learn these 3 high-frequency **-AR** verbs: **Hablar** (to speak), **Trabajar** (to work), **Necesitar** (to need).
2.  Practice "Lego Building": Say out loud: *"Yo necesito trabajar"* (I need to work) or *"Él habla ahora"* (He speaks now).

## 🧠 Meta-Learning & Separation Strategy

### 1. The "Negative Priming" Technique
To keep Japanese from "leaking" in on Thursday, we need to harden the Spanish shell. 
* **Today's Exercise:** Spend 2 minutes trying to think of a Japanese word, then immediately "slam the door" on it by shouting a Spanish word like **¡HOLA!** or **¡SÍ!**. This trains your brain that the Japanese "file" is locked until Thursday.

### 2. Deconstructing Syntax (The Mirror)
Spanish syntax is very similar to English (Subject-Verb-Object). 
* *English:* I want to eat something.
* *Spanish:* (Yo) quiero comer algo.
* **Note:** In Spanish, we often drop the "Yo" (I) because the verb ending **-o** already says "I". This is "Pro-Drop." Start practicing dropping the "Yo" today.
